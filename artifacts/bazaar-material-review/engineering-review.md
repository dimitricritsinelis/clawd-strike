# Bazaar R5 material engineering review

Read-only source review, 2026-09-09. Active issue is `R5-wall-by-wall-window-composition`, proposed, not game accepted. No design, runtime, or asset changes. Reproduce with `python3 artifacts/bazaar-material-review/inventory.py` and `python3 artifacts/bazaar-material-review/embedded-images.py` from the repository root. Inventory scope and caveats are embedded in the JSON outputs.

## Findings that affect the finish

1. **Use the craft paint recipe, not the original scan multiplied by owner paint.** `design.json` lines 87999–88043 already specifies the correct bounded linear formula and white ceramic base. The source is strongly colored: image-wide mean sRGB byte values are rough pine `[76,37,14]`, dark wood `[93,42,24]`, fabric leather `[129,78,26]`, hessian `[140,120,89]`. These are image statistics, not rendered colors. A cream tint cannot make the leather scan cream; multiplicative tint darkens it. The new warm timber `grainMix=0.28`, cloth `0.12`, opaque painted timber `0` avoid that trap while retaining restrained detail. Implement these existing requirements before proposing replacement scans.

2. **Resolve craft color ownership before authoring multicolored cloth.** `craftStandards.materials.exportRecipe` asks for area-owned baked paint/scan albedo; `accentRecipe` allows stock colors on a white base. If a cream-painted image is subsequently multiplied by a desired stock color, this reintroduces the double tint the contract forbids. A bounded neutral grain image `((1-g)+g*sourceLinear)` with desired linear vertex paint can support many cloth colors in one material. Alternatively bake the desired paint once and leave vertices white. Record exactly which path each shared material uses. Opaque timber and ceramics already explicitly use white base plus desired vertex color.

3. **Material schedule and implementation notes are stale relative to craft and runtime.** `materialRuntimeContract.currentFailure` says BZ04 profiles and floor order are absent. They exist: all 20 wall entries are in `apps/client/src/runtime/map/wallShaderProfiles.ts:7`, and all seven floors in `buildPbrFloors.ts:23,125,146`. Do not redo them or alter generic shaders. The 25-area schedule still lists old timber, ceramic and iron names rather than `bz04_craft_warm_timber`, `bz04_craft_ceramic`, etc. Existing budgets remain relevant, but a final resolved mapping must reconcile replacements, retained IDs and shadow splits before proving the limits. Current source generators contain no craft recipe implementation; the proposed R5 finish must not be inferred from installed B-04 models.

4. **Blender and runtime PBR are not fully equivalent.** `assets/source/facade_materials.py:54` correctly converts sRGB tint once and marks normal/ARM non-color. However it wires ARM blue directly to Metallic and multiplies its green roughness by the scalar. `WallMaterialLibrary.ts:372` sets metallic factor zero, so runtime pack metalness remains zero even with an ARM metalnessMap. Setting only the Blender Metallic default cannot override a connected texture; craft requires disconnecting that input. A craft roughness target must also specify whether it is a constant or a multiplier of the scan. For a desired matte finish, a factor of 0.86 is not a guaranteed final roughness of 0.86.

5. **Boost has different semantics in embedded versus pack material paths.** Blender multiplies and clamps tint by `albedoBoost`; runtime `applyWallShaderTweaks.ts:386` applies `c*f/(1+(f-1)*c)`. Most BZ04 architecture aliases use boost 1, so this is limited to boosted detail such as rusty metal/worn planks (1.18) and pine (1.03). Preserve explicit craft names so `_strip_pack_images` and runtime alias replacement cannot discard painted albedo. Check an exported ordinary glTF material, not a Blender viewport alone.

## Reuse and measured file costs

| Scope | IDs | Unique texture triplets at 1k | Encoded bytes | Estimated RGBA8 + mip bytes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| All installed wall + floor manifests | 84 | 38 | 63,521,736 | 624,852,954 |
| IDs referenced directly in proposed areas/skyline/ground | 23 | 18 | 27,696,796 | 289,308,654 |

The second row omits recipe-driven embedded details and retained runtime content; it is not a complete scene budget. Full-manifest 2k preference (53 IDs at 2k, 31 falling back to 1k) totals 144,339,863 encoded bytes and estimated 1,342,078,938 decoded bytes. The direct-reference subset at 2k totals 82,416,884 encoded and estimated 754,876,398 decoded bytes. These are unique-file inventories; neither all files on disk nor all manifest IDs are necessarily loaded.

- **Aliases are intentional reuse, not copied texture storage.** 68 wall IDs resolve to 33 1k triplets; 16 floor IDs resolve to five. `WallMaterialLibrary.ts:485` caches by resolved URL; normal/roughness/AO reuse the same ARM texture object. No byte-identical files at distinct texture-pack paths were found. Removing alias names would not produce proportional texture-memory savings.
- **Texture reuse can still look repetitive.** Seven plaster/whitewash aliases share `white_plaster_02`; six plaster/ochre aliases share `plastered_wall`; seven floor IDs share `medieval_blocks_05`. `patterned_cobblestone` and `court_limestone_flags_01` share their scan but tile at 2.6 versus 4.4 m in the BZ04 aliases. Preserve physical course sizes; aliases do not establish new paving geometry. Quiet fields can reuse a scan; identity should come from designed construction and scoped paint.
- **There is real embedded-image duplication on disk.** Across all 37 Bazaar GLBs, 175 embedded images contain 71,351,474 bytes; exact byte hashing yields 68 unique images containing 30,811,998 bytes, leaving 40,539,476 redundant copy bytes. This includes retained/superseded models and is not active residency or a guaranteed saving. The largest image payloads are B shared environment (11,513,720), B roof bundle (11,023,164), Rug Gate landmark (6,894,262), Rug Gate roof bundle (6,593,124). `embedded-images.json` identifies exact duplicate holders. First compare selected active GLBs, then consider sharing their identical images; no compression rollout is proposed.
- **Area-owned craft images trade simplicity for repeated payload.** One square 1024x1024 RGBA8 image with mips is about 5.33 MiB, three are 16 MiB; 2048 square is four times that. If all 25 areas independently embed one identical neutral cloth image, the upper-bound duplicate decoded image cost relative to one shared image is 128 MiB. This is an illustrative allocation estimate, not actual usage. Canonical source/formula/material reuse is a bounded future option; per-owner images/materials are unnecessary when vertex colors suffice.

## Batching and bounded follow-up

The 25 area allocations sum to 235 material slots, 260 rendered primitive ceilings, 166 shadow primitive ceilings and 748,000 triangles. These are sums of per-area limits, not unique global material counts or measured draws. Global new allocation is 291 rendered primitives/838,316 triangles, reserving 209 draws/461,684 triangles under 500/1,300,000 mobile ceilings.

Roof batching is already implemented in `buildFacadeModels.ts:128`: the contract reduces 88 prebatch primitives to at most 11 by material name, geometry attribute signature and shadow class. This is a planned maximum reduction of 77 primitive draws, not a measured result. Keep that path and per-area batching. Avoid a material per window, hem, stock color or pot. The pack material cache inside `rebindPackMaterials` is per model, so equal IDs across area models share textures but not necessarily material instances; cross-area material caching could reduce object overhead only after proving profile/seed/floor/shadow equivalence. It does not by itself merge geometry or remove draw calls.

A minor loader consistency issue exists: wall preload requests normal/ARM anisotropy 8, while lazy `applyMaps` requests 1; first URL-cache load wins. Inspect actual load order before changing this. It affects grazing-angle detail, not proof of a global FPS issue.

Before construction, reconcile the material mapping and cloth color recipe. During the first bounded build, inspect exported base-color factors, vertex colors and Metallic connections, then review neutral daylight and sun/shade player views. Actual renderer texture residency, draw calls, shadow draws and frame time remain unmeasured. No automated inventory proves the golden sun-faded appearance.

## Bounded provenance and proposed palette footprint

`provenance-check.py` verifies actual bytes against adjacent `sources.json` tier/channel MD5 records for the wall/floor packs plus path-based textile/palm records. **177 file records match; zero missing files, missing hashes, missing pack image records, ambiguous matches or mismatches were found.** This checks local records, not upstream authorship. Windows `SOURCE.md` and model provenance are outside this check.

Not all source textures are scans. The floor families `court_flagstone_01` and `sand_drift_01` declare `repo://generated/bazaar/floors/...` and `Project-Original`. The proposed quiet flags use the former. Textiles and the frond cutout are also project-original; external wall/floor families carry Poly Haven CC0 records. Preserve these distinctions in a final implementation source ledger.

The 16-family proposed palette resolves to **33 unique source files**, including the unmodified rug and only the two needed non-albedo maps for opaque painted timber: **18,957,041 encoded bytes** and **184,451,061 estimated RGBA8+mip bytes**. The current direct area/skyline/ground-reference subset contains 54 files, 27,696,796 encoded bytes and 289,308,654 estimated decoded bytes. The raw differences are 21 files, 8,739,755 encoded bytes and 104,857,593 estimated decoded bytes. They are **not projected whole-scene savings**: existing trim, roofs, retained props, unseen recipe materials, future derived bakes and repeated embeddings remain outside this comparison. The proposed set adds the rug to this direct-reference scope; all its other source files already exist in the current subset. See `palette-footprint.json` for exact mappings and overlap.
