# R5 material art review

Status: proposal only. Geometry, active handoff, runtime and source assets are unchanged. This review inspected the actual founding Bazaar and CS2 daylight images, every albedo referenced by the 27 scheduled aliases, and the existing Levantine rug. The three adjacent contact sheets show raw source images with scheduled repeat/tint labels; they are not rendered final materials.

## Findings in priority order

1. **The wall schedule's sandy swatches conceal a dark source.** `ph_bz04_beige_wall_002` references a 1024×256 brown albedo averaging approximately RGB 131/111/85. Multiplying it by `#d5c1a0` cannot produce the pale sand pictured by that swatch. It covers many domestic, merchant and service owners. Propose using the existing quieter `plastered_wall` source as a shared sand-plaster family. Its source average is approximately 176/168/156. Calibrate desired appearance on an actual wall sample; do not brighten the whole scene.
2. **Several source identities conflict with the written material roles.** `fabric_leather_02` visibly contains orange leather and a central seam, not linen weave. `whitewashed_brick_warm/cool` both use mottled gray `white_plaster_02`, with no visible brick. `stone_trim_white` and `trim_sanded_01` use a bricklike masonry scan with mortar lines, not sanded monolithic stone. The latter creates false joints across a modeled sill or arch piece. Keep source reuse, but assign it honestly. For one-piece trim, sample a mortar-free stone patch with verified local UVs; never apply a complete wall repeat across it.
3. **The intended R5 craft formula already fixes much of the raw-source darkness.** Timber grainMix 0.28, cloth 0.12, iron 0.12, opaque painted joinery and direct ceramic colors are materially better than raw scan × dark tint. Retain them. Leather remains a physical-surface mismatch even after its orange color is suppressed; use the existing hessian weave for the first neutral cloth sample. Warm timber must retain grain within each modeled board, not inherit additional photographed seams across rails.
4. **The floor family is the biggest broad-field brown/noise risk.** `large_sandstone_blocks_01`, `spice_laid_stone_01` and `cobblestone_color` all reference the same `medieval_blocks_05` rubble source, with large irregular pale stones, brown mortar and strong local contrast. Repeating it at 2.6, 3.6 and 3.1 m changes apparent unit size without creating a genuinely different paving tradition. The `court_flagstone_01` source is quieter and better suited to the main court/trade route readability target. Reuse it more broadly with a consistent trial repeat; keep the rough source for the service circuit. The red sandstone source is dark gray-brown rather than sun-faded red. The link cobblestone source has conspicuous green moss in joints, which is a poor fit for general swept dry links.
5. **Several same-building finish changes need a named construction reason or one shared field material.** Strong existing coherent examples: Textile East/West with their rear returns, guildhall, B tea, B receiving store and Hammam. Corrections proposed in JSON: A Spice West return, A Spice East warehouse return, Spice East North/F_SE, red household/F_SW, Fountain corner house/Souk reverse, Dogleg annex/North Court return, North drying compound and Dyers East. Preserve all modeled stone bases and sills. A retaining portion such as the Rug Merchant stair-side face may properly stay stone below grade; the proposal concerns the exposed occupied-building wall field, not a blind recolor of buried retaining fabric.

## Curated appearance

The founding image genuinely supports golden stone/plaster, dark-to-mid timber, teal shutters and signs, rust cloth and a shared warm ground. It is an illustrative, warmly lit scene with deep brown shadows; its exposure/grade and debris are misleading as final material targets. The CS2 image genuinely supports pale warm paving, quiet wall fields, legible massing and crisp shade. Copying its brightest lit pixels as base colors would create the white/sterile result the user rejects.

Use cream plaster (`#d8c4a0`), sand plaster (`#cbb38b`) and pale lime (`#ddd0b3`) as **proposed desired appearances**, with ochre (`#c6a16c`) for the dye-work owners and the existing muted red plaster (`#b77c62`) for the merchant/red household identities. These are not tint multipliers. Keep the guildhall, Hammam, gateway and robust store masonry. Keep the retained R5 sage-teal (`#78978d`) and indigo (`#66778c`) and rust (`#ae654c`) in existing joinery and goods. The inspected rug already unifies these colors with flax and should remain unmodified. Do not introduce a new rug pattern or a new accent on every building.

The adjacent JSON contains a proposal for every registered building/assembly and every zone floor. It uses shared source families rather than a material quota. Same-owner corners should carry one plaster/stone story; neighboring owners may change at their existing property joint. The Dyers East fields should read as one ochre work compound with preserved wet-work bases, rather than stone/ochre/cream strips merely corresponding to drafting parcels. The two continuous Textile buildings should keep their current coherent cream/pale-lime relationship and let existing goods supply the color.

## Scale and wear

At the scheduled 2 m repeat, dressed sandstone has roughly 0.2 m courses, a plausible restrained masonry scale. The rustic service source has many thin stacked stones and can evoke modern stacked-stone cladding if spread to prominent whole buildings; retain it selectively on robust service owners rather than using it for added variation. The wide beige source needs explicit aspect-aware treatment if retained; the local file is non-square, so physical capture dimensions and UV orientation need confirmation before a square-repeat rule is accepted.

Keep maintained local contact/use wear. Disable neither source inspection nor the existing bounded wear logic: source albedos already contain mottling, oxidation, damage, seams and moss. Generic dirt-disabled settings do not erase these. The `worn_plaster_wall` niche source contains severe peeling and a vertical edge/seam; the owning quiet plaster is a better recess backing unless a specific damaged patch is intended. White-plaster source mottling should not become the default full-height gray grime. Neutral grain maps and quiet source reuse are preferable to painting more dirt over them.

## Evidence limits

This is an image and source-schedule review, not a final PBR render or material approval. Raw albedo appearance, a diagram fill and texture × tint × vertex color × lighting are different things. R5 craft-painted surfaces additionally use a bounded linear-space formula, so raw sheets exaggerate their intended darkness. Proposed target colors need one neutral-light and one shipped-light built sample before exact tint values are frozen. No old window signoff establishes material acceptance.


Primary-source spot checks: [Poly Haven leather](https://polyhaven.com/a/fabric_leather_02) describes stitched upholstery, and [the cobblestone source](https://polyhaven.com/a/cobblestone_floor_04) describes moss-filled joints. The local court_flagstone_01 source is Project-Original, not a Poly Haven scan. The [beige-wall page](https://polyhaven.com/a/beige_wall_002) lists a 3 m capture dimension; the local aspect/UV orientation remains to be reconciled rather than assuming pixel dimensions alone prove physical distortion.
